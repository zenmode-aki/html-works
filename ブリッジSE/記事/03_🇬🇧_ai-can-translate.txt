"Why not just translate it with AI?" You would think that, right 🐧

It's true that in the past, documents had to be translated by people.
But these days we have AI.

AI can translate.

So "what am I even doing here" was a question
that stayed with me, honestly, the whole time I was working there.

For something like live interpreting in a meeting,
there may still be some point in a person doing it.

But translating documents? A machine can seriously just do that.

Still, it was because I worked hard at it
that I noticed early on: this is going to be replaced by AI.

That got me thinking.

I thought it would be better to work on technical skill than on language skill.

It's not so much that I can't speak English.
In my case, anything I can't say in Japanese, I can't say in English either.

If I can't say it in my own language, I can't say it in English.

So I thought I should make the technical side solid first.

I did have a vague feeling that I wanted to work globally,
but a Japanese company would be completely fine too.

Still, I like English, so it would be nice
to work somewhere I can use English even a little.
That's the vague direction I have.

But to add value in a place where English is used,
the words have to come to me first. Even in Japanese.

And for that, if I don't properly understand the technology,
I probably can't contribute much in any environment at all.

For the me of 5 years, 6 years, 7 years from now
to do well overseas, or somewhere English is used,
technical skill is what matters more. I felt that strongly.

That "uh oh" moment is what decided the direction I'm going now.
