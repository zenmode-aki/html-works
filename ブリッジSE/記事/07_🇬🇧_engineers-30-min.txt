I got seriously scolded once, just for wanting to chat 🐧

Before that, let me give you the background.

It was a multinational team, but the client, the president
and my boss were all Japanese.
The person at the very top was Japanese.

It didn't really feel that way in the office day to day,
but the Japanese side and the Filipino side do split a little.
An air of "you go along with what the Japanese side says" does form.

And this wasn't only that company.
It was the same when I was an intern at a language school in the Philippines.
The school had been founded by Korean owners,
so it was the same kind of thing: you follow their rules.

The engineers there listened to us really well,
and they properly took our opinions on board.

But exactly because of that, I felt a wall:
"maybe they are a little afraid of us, because we're Japanese."

And if that wall is there, then when something urgent happens,
or an error comes up, they might not escalate it to me.
That was what scared me the most.

So I wanted to build a relationship where we could talk frankly,
and I set up a 30-minute chat, called "Casual MTG."

After it ended, a senior bridge SE called me over.

"The engineers' time is time the client is paying for."
"If that 30 minutes delays the work, the client is the one who suffers."

Before that, I used to think, lightly,
that if we missed a deadline, we could just work overtime.

Man-days, man-months. I used to think they were just units.
But they are an engineer's 8 hours a day, literally.

Since then, whenever workload comes up, I straighten up a little.
