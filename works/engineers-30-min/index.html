🌉 BRIDGE ENGINEER⚡ 15 SEC

An engineer's 30 minutes is time the client is paying for 🐧

😳 The Scolding

I got seriously scolded once, just for wanting to chat.

🗺 Some Background First

Before that, let me give you the background.

🏢 All Japanese

It was a multinational team, but the client, the president and my boss were all Japanese. The person at the very top was Japanese.

🪞 A Small Split

It didn't really feel that way in the office day to day, but the Japanese side and the Filipino side do split a little. An air of "you go along with what the Japanese side says" does form.

🏫 Not Just Here

And this wasn't only that company. It was the same when I was an intern at a language school in the Philippines. The school had been founded by Korean owners, so it was the same kind of thing: you follow their rules.

👂 They Listened Well

The engineers there listened to us really well, and they properly took our opinions on board.

🧱 The Wall

But exactly because of that, I felt a wall: "maybe they are a little afraid of us, because we're Japanese."

🚨 What Scared Me

And if that wall is there, then when something urgent happens, or an error comes up, they might not escalate it to me. That was what scared me the most.

☕ Casual MTG

So I wanted to build a relationship where we could talk frankly, and I set up a 30-minute chat, called "Casual MTG."

🙇 Called Me Over

After it ended, a senior bridge SE called me over.

💬 What He Said

"The engineers' time is time the client is paying for." "If that 30 minutes delays the work, the client is the one who suffers."

🌙 My Old Thinking

Before that, I used to think, lightly, that if we missed a deadline, we could just work overtime.

⏳ 8 Hours Daily

Man-days, man-months. I used to think they were just units. But they are an engineer's 8 hours a day, literally.

🎯 I Straighten Up

Since then, whenever workload comes up, I straighten up a little.
